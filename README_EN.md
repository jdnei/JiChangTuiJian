<a id="purchasing"></a>

> 💡 **Read Before You Buy**
>
> * Start with a monthly or short-term plan instead of paying for a long subscription upfront.
> * Do not judge a VPN only by its price or server count; **bandwidth, reliability, and route quality** matter more.
> * **[Relay / Dedicated Line](#premium) generally** performs better than basic direct connections, but results still depend on your region and internet provider (China Mobile, China Unicom, China Telecom, or China Broadnet).
> * If you use a VPN often, **always keep a [backup VPN](#value)**; do not depend on a single provider for everything.
> * For specialized needs (Emby streaming, Xinjiang access, unlimited devices, or unlimited data), **[specialty VPNs](#specialty) are often** a better fit.

<details>

<br>

* **How should I choose between direct, relay, and dedicated-line connections?**
  In general, I recommend relay-based VPNs over basic direct connections. Relays usually offer more bandwidth; dedicated lines can avoid some Great Firewall interference and reduce latency, making everyday use smoother. Do not rely on labels like “dedicated” or “relay,” though—actual route quality is what matters.

* **What makes a VPN service good?**
  Ultimately, VPN quality comes down to infrastructure. Speed depends mainly on total bandwidth; reliability depends on upstream routes and relay servers; streaming access depends on exit servers and unblocking providers. Overall, **bandwidth and reliability matter most**.

* **Should I worry about the protocol?**
  For relay-based VPNs, the protocol is usually not the most important factor. The relay server’s stability and available bandwidth often affect real-world performance more than the protocol choice.

* **Why should I not buy solely based on rankings?**
  Performance varies by region, internet provider, and even time of day. Test a short-term plan first; the best VPN is the one that works for you.
